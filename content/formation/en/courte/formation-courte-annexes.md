# Dropshipping Course — Short version: practical appendices

## Appendix A — Launch checklist for a new product

Before launching a campaign on a new product, check in order: the exact product cost at the chosen supplier, shipping included; the real transaction fees of your payment processor; the import duties and taxes of the delivery country, including the European tax of 3 euros per item category for a parcel coming from outside the EU; an acquisition cost estimated prudently, from historical data if you have any; an assumed return rate consistent with the product category, never assumed to be zero; the contributions or taxes applicable to your status, calculated on revenue and not on margin. Only launch the campaign if the net margin from this complete calculation stays positive with a reasonable safety margin, not just at exact break-even.

Also check, on the supplier side, that you've compared at least two or three candidates on their average rating and volume of orders already placed — exactly what needs comparing — and that a test order confirmed the real quality before any volume commitment.

## Appendix B — Condensed audit grid for a product page

A product page that converts meets six minimum requirements, inspired by the public work of the Nielsen Norman Group and the Baymard Institute on the online shopping experience: a first image in a real usage context, copy organized around the visitor's questions rather than technical specs alone, at least one honest limitation acknowledged in the copy, a total price visible with no hidden calculation, reassurance elements placed near the buy button rather than at the bottom of the page, and reviews presented with realistic nuance rather than suspicious perfection. Dropvio's built-in landing page auditor automatically checks this kind of criteria — a score out of a hundred across nine criteria, including the mobile version — before a page receives paid ad traffic.

## Appendix C — A margin calculation worked through in full

Take a niche product with a supplier cost of 9.50 euros, sold for 34.90 euros. Transaction fees (about 1.5% + 0.25 euro with a European card): about 0.77 euro. European tax on a parcel shipped from outside the EU: 3 euros. Estimated acquisition cost: 9 euros. That leaves 12.63 euros before contributions and returns. With an assumed return rate of 12%, consistent with a more technical product where the customer can get compatibility wrong, the real average margin drops to about 11.11 euros. URSSAF contributions as a micro-enterprise, at about 12.3% of revenue collected, represent about 4.29 euros. Final net margin: about 6.82 euros per sale, a little under 20% of the selling price. This same reasoning, applied to each of your products with your own real figures, must come before every campaign launch, without exception.

## Appendix D — Signals to watch once the business is launched

A slight, gradual rise in the return rate on a specific product, even if small each month in isolation, deserves to be tracked over time rather than ignored. A gradual lengthening of a supplier's delivery time can signal a broader decline to come. A margin that slowly erodes without any single item exploding is often the cumulative result of several small simultaneous declines, which only regular tracking of the real indicators can spot rather than an occasional check. A lengthening customer service response time almost always precedes a rise in the dispute rate with a lag of a few weeks. Growing dependence on a single acquisition channel exposes the whole business to any unfavorable change in that channel.

## Appendix E — Essential glossary

Net margin: what really remains after deducting all costs (product, transaction, import taxes, acquisition, returns, contributions), as opposed to gross margin, which often deducts only the product cost. Customer acquisition cost (CAC): the average amount spent on advertising to get one paying customer. Cross-selling: offering a complementary product at the time of the main purchase, which improves the margin without adding extra acquisition cost. Dispute (chargeback): a challenge of the payment made directly with the customer's bank, the most serious situation among return/refund/dispute since it's judged by a third party and can threaten access to your payment processing. Micro-enterprise: a French simplified regime with contributions calculated on revenue collected. IOSS: a European scheme simplifying VAT collection on low-value shipments.
