# Part 29 — The very first customers: what counts differently

The very first sales of a new store deserve different attention from that given once the business is up and running, because each of these first customer experiences weighs proportionally much more on the store's budding reputation than one sale among hundreds once the business is established.

## Why each first customer counts more than the average

On a store that has just started, the very first reviews, the very first informal recommendations or criticisms, and the very first signs of satisfaction or dissatisfaction don't yet have enough mass to average out statistically — one unhappy customer among the first five weighs proportionally far more than one unhappy customer among five hundred. That justifies a level of attention and responsiveness in customer service, detailed in the corresponding part of this course, even higher for these very first customers than for the business average once established.

## Treat each first piece of feedback as a source of learning, not just a transaction

Each piece of feedback, positive or negative, obtained on these first sales is precious information about the real quality of the product page, the chosen supplier, and the buying journey built — far more precious than any hypothesis formulated before launch. A seller who takes the time to actively ask for honest feedback on these first orders, rather than simply hoping everything goes well in silence, considerably speeds up the useful adjustments mentioned in the audit grid in the part on the store that converts.

## Don't draw premature statistical conclusions

Conversely, a symmetrical trap is drawing definitive conclusions from a still very small number of sales — an excellent conversion rate on the first five visits, or on the contrary a disappointing rate, says nothing reliable yet about the product's real performance once a more significant volume is reached. It's the same principle already detailed for ad acquisition: wait for enough data before judging, without ignoring the individual qualitative signals these very first pieces of feedback provide, which remain valuable regardless of volume.

## The disproportionate role of these first customers in future trust

The very first reviews published on a product page or on the store as a whole directly influence the trust of the following visitors, at a time when the store has no history yet to reassure them otherwise. Taking particular care of these very first experiences, including sometimes accepting a goodwill gesture that wouldn't systematically be justified later once the business is established, is an investment in the starting reputation whose value far exceeds its immediate cost.
