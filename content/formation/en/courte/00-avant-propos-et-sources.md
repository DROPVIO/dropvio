# Foreword

This short version isn't a superficial summary of the long version — it's a course in its own right, built to give the essentials of each topic in a tighter format, for those who want to start quickly on solid foundations, even if it means going deeper on certain points later in the long version. Every part keeps the same demand for rigor: when a figure isn't confirmed by a verifiable primary source, it's presented as an approximation widely quoted in the industry, never as an established fact.

The mentions of Dropvio features throughout the text aren't advertising inserted out of context — they're tools that concretely apply, with less risk of execution errors, the method detailed in these pages.

## Sources

The principles of conversion and shopping experience draw on the public methodological frameworks of the Nielsen Norman Group and the Baymard Institute, two recognized references in research on usability and e-commerce cart abandonment. The French regulatory elements (micro-enterprise, VAT, IOSS, legal obligations) draw on the official resources service-public.fr, the French tax website and URSSAF — a framework that changes regularly and that you should check when you actually need it, not memorize for good from this reading. The technical elements on the AliExpress and Shopify APIs draw on their official public developer documentation. The general statistics on store failure rates and the difficulties most often cited by sellers come from content widely circulated in the e-commerce industry, without a clearly verifiable primary academic study behind them — they're therefore systematically presented as approximate orders of magnitude rather than confirmed data.
