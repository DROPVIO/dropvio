# Part 26 — Reading a month of cash flow through a worked example

A store collects 8,200 euros in revenue over a month — an isolated figure that looks excellent, but says nothing about what's really available. Once product cost, import taxes and transaction fees (about 3,400 euros), the ad budget spent (2,300 euros), refunds processed (410 euros) and subscriptions/tools (140 euros) are deducted, about 1,950 euros remain before contributions. The URSSAF contributions to set aside on this collected revenue represent about 1,009 euros — to put aside immediately rather than discover at declaration time. About 941 euros remain really available for this particular month, and it's this amount, not the initial 8,200 euros, that should guide any decision to reinvest or put money in reserve.

The next month, a supplier lengthening its delivery time can push the refund rate on a product up from 8% to 15% within a few weeks — a signal gross revenue never shows directly. A safety reserve built up over previous months absorbs this shock while you identify the problem and respond to it, for example by reassessing that supplier or activating the already qualified backup supplier. The amounts used here are a teaching example, but the principle remains universal: it's what remains after deducting, in order, product costs, transaction fees, advertising, refunds, tools and contributions that should guide management decisions — never the gross revenue displayed first.

# Part 27 — Administrative checklist before the first launch

*These guidelines are general and don't replace checking your specific situation with an accountant.*

Before creating the store: check the appropriate legal status (the micro-enterprise in the vast majority of cases at the start), and start registering the business early, taking into account the administrative delay for obtaining the SIRET number, which can't be compressed — a delay that also affects activating a payment collection solution. Before going live: write terms and conditions of sale suited to your actual business, put in place a GDPR-compliant privacy policy, display a clear legal notice, and state the terms of the right of withdrawal consistently with what's actually applied internally — a gap between what's written and actual practice is a frequent source of avoidable disputes.

On VAT: check whether the exemption still applies at your revenue level, and look into the IOSS scheme for sales to other European countries on low-value shipments, as well as the European tax of 3 euros per item category on parcels under 150 euros coming from outside the EU. On an ongoing basis: set aside contributions as soon as money comes in, and review your tax situation as soon as a significant revenue threshold is reached, anticipating rather than reacting after the fact. Each of these points may seem secondary next to the urgency of launching the store — it's precisely this inverted hierarchy that explains why so many sellers have to urgently regularize a situation they could have handled calmly from the start.
