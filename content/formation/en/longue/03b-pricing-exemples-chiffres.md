# Part 3 (supplement) — Three margin calculations worked through in full

The principles in Part 3 stay abstract until you apply them to real figures. Here are three complete examples, from supplier price to final net margin, on three different product profiles — a low-priced loss leader, a mid-range product, and a premium bundle. The goal is for you to be able to reproduce exactly this reasoning on your own products.

All three examples assume a sale in France, as a micro-enterprise under the VAT exemption threshold, with a supplier shipping from a non-EU country: each parcel therefore bears the European flat tax of 3 euros per item category in force since July 1, 2026 (see Part 3).

## Example 1 — The low-priced loss leader

An accessory costs 3.20 euros at the supplier, supplier shipping included. It sells for 14.90 euros in the store.

First item: the product cost, 3.20 euros, raises no questions — it's the starting point. Second item, transaction fees: counting about 1.5% plus a fixed 0.25 euro on a 14.90-euro transaction (the rate for a European card; count closer to 2.5% for a card issued outside the EU), this item comes to about 0.47 euro. On a low-priced product, that fixed 0.25 euro alone already weighs more than 1.5% — exactly the trap mentioned in Part 3 about loss leaders.

Third item, the European tax on the parcel: 3 euros, or 20% of the selling price on its own. Fourth item, advertising acquisition: assuming a customer acquisition cost of 4 euros on this kind of low-priced product (a figure to adjust to your own real ad performance, never to copy as is), what remains is: 14.90 − 3.20 − 0.47 − 3.00 − 4.00 = 4.23 euros before contributions and before returns.

Fifth item, an assumed return rate of 8% on this kind of product (simple accessory, few size-related reasons for return): this rate weighs on the real average margin across all sales, not only on the returned orders, since each return wipes out the entire margin of that particular sale. Out of a hundred sales at 4.23 euros of margin before returns, eight come back to zero (or below, if the transaction fees on the refund aren't recovered), bringing the real average margin to around 3.89 euros per sale overall.

Sixth item, URSSAF contributions as a micro-enterprise, around 12.3% of revenue collected (14.90 euros here, not the margin): about 1.83 euros per sale. Approximate final net margin: 3.89 − 1.83 = about 2.06 euros per sale, a little under 14% of the selling price. The product stays barely positive, but the slightest rise in acquisition cost tips it into the red: at this price level, a loss leader hardly justifies itself on its own, only if it triggers a complementary sale or a second purchase — a figure to check campaign by campaign, never assumed in advance.

## Example 2 — The mid-range product

A niche product (for example a technical accessory) costs 9.50 euros at the supplier and sells for 34.90 euros.

Product cost: 9.50 euros. Transaction fees (1.5% + 0.25 euro on 34.90): about 0.77 euro. European tax on the parcel: 3 euros. Estimated acquisition cost, higher on this kind of product that needs more explanation before purchase: 9 euros. What remains: 34.90 − 9.50 − 0.77 − 3.00 − 9.00 = 12.63 euros before contributions and returns.

Assumed return rate of 12% on this more technical kind of product, where the customer can get compatibility or use wrong: real average margin down to about 11.11 euros. URSSAF contributions at 12.3% of revenue: about 4.29 euros. Final net margin: about 6.82 euros per sale, a little under 20% of the selling price — a higher percentage than the first example, and an absolute amount per sale more than three times higher: the fixed 3-euro tax weighs much less on a higher selling price, which shows how the same fixed cost can make a cheap product non-viable while remaining bearable on a more expensive one.

## Example 3 — The premium bundle

A main product costing 11 euros at the supplier, paired with a complementary accessory costing 2.50 euros at the supplier (the cross-selling technique seen in Part 4), sold together for 59.90 euros instead of two separate purchases that would have cost the customer more.

Total product cost: 13.50 euros. Transaction fees (1.5% + 0.25 euro on 59.90): about 1.15 euros. European tax: the parcel contains two items in different categories, so we prudently count 2 × 3 = 6 euros. Acquisition cost: this cost stays identical to that of a single sale since it's the same customer acquired once — exactly the advantage of cross-selling detailed in Part 4. Using an acquisition cost of 9 euros as in example 2: 59.90 − 13.50 − 1.15 − 6.00 − 9.00 = 30.25 euros before contributions and returns.

With a 10% return rate: real average margin around 27.23 euros. URSSAF contributions at 12.3% of 59.90 euros: about 7.37 euros. Final net margin: about 19.86 euros per sale, about 33% of the selling price — far higher in percentage AND in absolute value than the two previous examples, for an advertising acquisition cost identical to that of a single sale. That's the numerical demonstration of why cross-selling, mentioned in Part 4, is one of the most profitable levers in all of dropshipping: it adds almost no extra cost to the most expensive item in the calculation, acquisition.

## Checklist before launching a campaign on a new product

Check the exact product cost at the chosen supplier, shipping included. Calculate the real transaction fees of your payment processor, not a rough estimate. Include the import duties and taxes applicable to the delivery country, in particular the European tax of 3 euros per item category for a parcel shipped from outside the EU. Estimate a realistic acquisition cost from your own historical data if you have any, or a prudent assumption if it's a first test. Apply an assumed return rate consistent with the product category, rather than assuming zero. Include the contributions or taxes applicable to your status on revenue, not on margin. Only launch the campaign if the net margin from this complete calculation stays positive with a reasonable safety margin, not just at exact break-even.
