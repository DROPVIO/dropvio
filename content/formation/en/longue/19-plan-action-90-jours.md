# Part 19 — 90-day action plan

This part sums up the whole course as a concrete plan, week after week, for a structured launch over the first three months. This calendar remains indicative — every situation moves at its own pace — but it gives a logical order to follow rather than a duration to stick to day by day.

## Weeks 1 to 2 — Product research and validation

Narrow a broad idea into several concrete variants, as detailed in Part 2 and its case study. Check the search volume and the trend over time of each variant. Compare at least two or three potential suppliers on their average rating and order volume. Place a test order with the chosen supplier before any volume commitment.

## Weeks 3 to 4 — Building the store and the financial calculation

Calculate the complete net margin of the chosen product with the built-in calculator, including all the items detailed in Part 3. Build the product page following the audit grid in Part 4, check how it renders on mobile, then have it checked by the landing page auditor before going live for good. Put in place the mandatory legal documents mentioned in Part 9 — terms and conditions of sale, privacy policy, legal notice. Open a bank account dedicated to the business, in line with Part 8.

## Weeks 5 to 8 — First acquisition test and adjustments

Launch a first limited ad budget tier, following the logic of Part 6. Reply to every customer request promptly, using the adaptable reply templates in Part 7. Set aside, from the very first receipts, the share corresponding to upcoming social contributions, in line with Part 8. Reassess the results of the first tier on sufficient data volume before deciding on a higher second tier or an adjustment to the product page or targeting.

## Weeks 9 to 12 — Consolidation and first additions to the range

Identify one or two complementary products consistent with the initial product, following the complementarity criteria in Part 2. Test a first bundle offer based on the numerical reasoning in Part 3 on cross-selling. Set up regular weekly tracking of key indicators, as detailed in Part 18. If the store is connected through Shopify, set up weekly tracking of refunds and disputes rather than only checking them when in doubt.

## Beyond the first quarter

Once this first three-month cycle is complete, the principles don't change — they repeat, product after product, with a demand for rigor that must never slacken under the effect of accumulated experience or the pressure of volume, exactly as detailed in Part 10 on what must stay stable while the rest of the business grows. It's the disciplined repetition of this cycle, more than any one-off trick, that builds a dropshipping business able to last beyond its first year — the period when, as recalled from Part 1, most stores launched stop.
