# Part 26 — Second case study: a warning signal caught in time

The case study in Part 11 followed a store over a whole year, with difficulties overcome thanks to upfront preparation. This second case, tighter in time, focuses on a single critical episode — a seasonal product with a short selling window — to show how a weak signal, caught in time thanks to the regular tracking detailed in Part 18, avoids what could have been a significant loss. As with the previous cases, it's a composite example built for teaching purposes.

## The context: a product with a short selling window

A store decides to add to its range a strongly seasonal product, whose demand is concentrated over about six weeks of the year, as detailed in Part 25. The product was validated using the method in Part 2 — search volume in the intermediate zone, underlying trend confirmed on data from previous years rather than an isolated spike — and a supplier was compared and chosen on reliability several weeks before the selling window opened, anticipating precisely the risk of longer supplier delays in periods of high demand mentioned in Part 25.

## The first signal, discreet, spotted during the weekly review

Two weeks after the campaign on this product launches, the weekly review of indicators, held with the same regularity as for the rest of the catalog in line with Part 18, reveals a detail that could easily go unnoticed if observed only once in isolation: the return rate on this specific product is slightly higher than the assumption used in the initial margin calculation from Part 3 — 14% observed against 9% anticipated. Taken alone over one week, this gap could be put down to the chance of a small sales volume. But the same review, the following week, confirms the trend rather than contradicting it.

## Investigation rather than immediate reaction

Rather than immediately cutting the campaign on this product or ignoring the signal by telling itself that the margin remains positive overall, the store takes the time, using the customer service feedback detailed in Part 7, to identify the precise cause of these more frequent than expected returns: several customers mention a noticeable difference between the dimensions announced on the product page and the product received — a gap which, once checked directly on a unit received by the store itself, turns out to be real although minor.

## The correction, quick, before the selling window closes

The product page is immediately corrected with precise dimensions and an additional image giving a clear scale of comparison, directly applying the audit grid in Part 4. The return rate falls to 8% within the following two weeks, even below the initial assumption. The product continues to sell for the rest of its seasonal window with a margin in line with the initial calculation, once this source of dissatisfaction, identified in time, is corrected.

## What this case shows, beyond this specific product

What made it possible to avoid a significant loss on this short-window product was neither luck nor exceptional intuition, but the disciplined application of two principles already detailed separately in this course: a regular review of indicators that makes a still-minor gap visible, and a methodical investigation of the real cause rather than an impulsive reaction — cutting the product without understanding why, or on the contrary ignoring it out of optimism. On a product with such a short selling window, each week lost ignoring the signal would have represented a disproportionate share of the whole selling period available in the year — which makes speed of detection even more decisive than on a product sold year-round, where a late correction can still be made up over the following months.
