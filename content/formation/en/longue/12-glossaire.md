# Part 12 — Complete glossary of dropshipping terms

This glossary brings together the technical terms used throughout this course, in the order in which they're useful to know rather than in strict alphabetical order within each theme. It can serve as a quick reference once you've finished the course.

## Terms related to the business model

Dropshipping refers to a sales model where the seller never physically holds the stock: the customer's order triggers a corresponding order at a supplier, who ships directly to the end customer. Net margin refers to what really remains after deducting all costs — product, transaction, acquisition, returns, contributions — as opposed to gross margin, which often deducts only the product cost and gives a misleadingly optimistic picture. Customer acquisition cost, often abbreviated CAC, refers to the average amount spent on advertising to get one paying customer.

## Terms related to the product and the supplier

A loss leader refers to a low-priced product used to attract a first purchase, sometimes with a reduced unit margin, with a view to then selling higher-margin complementary products. Cross-selling refers to offering a complementary product at the time of the main purchase, a technique detailed in Part 4. A backup supplier refers to a second supplier qualified for a given product, not actively used, but ready to take over if the main supplier fails — a precaution recommended in Part 5.

## Terms related to payment and incidents

A return refers to the customer physically sending a product back. A refund refers to giving the customer their money back, whether or not it follows a physical return. A dispute, or chargeback, refers to a challenge of the payment made directly with the customer's bank or payment method, without going through the seller — the most serious of the three, detailed in Part 7, because it's judged by a third party and can threaten access to your payment processing if it happens too often.

## Terms related to acquisition and conversion

Conversion rate refers to the proportion of visitors to a page who complete a purchase. A landing page refers to the page a visitor arrives on after clicking an ad, generally a product page optimized for that specific purpose. Cost per mille, or CPM, refers to the average cost for a thousand impressions of an ad, an indicator distinct from the final acquisition cost which, for its part, also depends on the conversion rate of the page the ad points to.

## Terms related to the French legal and tax framework

The micro-enterprise, formerly called "auto-entrepreneur", refers to a simplified regime for creating and running an individual business, with contributions calculated on revenue collected rather than a fixed lump sum. IOSS, the Import One-Stop Shop, refers to a European scheme simplifying VAT collection on low-value shipments from non-EU countries. The legal notice ("mentions légales") refers to the mandatory information identifying the operator of a commercial website, distinct from the terms and conditions of sale which govern the contractual terms of the relationship with the customer.

## Terms related to the structure of a growing store

Diversification, in the context of this course, refers to extending a business beyond a single product or a single acquisition channel, with the explicit aim of spreading risk rather than depending on a single point of failure. A brand, as opposed to generic dropshipping, refers to a differentiated identity — packaging, tone, a tight product selection — that sets a store apart from any competitor able to sell exactly the same generic product.
