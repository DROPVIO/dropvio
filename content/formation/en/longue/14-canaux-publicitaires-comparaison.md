# Part 14 — Comparing ad channels: what really sets them apart

Part 6 laid out the general principles of acquisition. This part details the concrete differences between the main channels available to a dropshipping seller, to help you choose where to focus a first test rather than spreading a limited budget over too many channels at once.

## Advertising on highly visual social networks

Platforms built around short visual content naturally favor products that are understood immediately from an image or a few seconds of video — a product whose use or appeal needs a long written explanation suits them less well than a product whose effect is visible at a glance. Targeting there works largely by interests and behavior, with an algorithm that gradually optimizes delivery as it accumulates conversion data — which explains why a test budget that's too small or too short often prevents the algorithm from reaching a truly meaningful optimization phase, a point to keep in mind when setting the minimum duration of a test tier mentioned in Part 6.

## Search engine advertising

Unlike social networks, search engine advertising targets an intent already expressed by the user when typing a query — a visitor actively searching for a type of product is generally closer to buying than one who discovers the same product by chance in a social feed. This channel suits products for which explicit search demand already exists particularly well, but it's structurally less suited to new or little-known products, which no one is yet spontaneously searching for.

## Organic content and creators

An organic presence built over time, or a partnership with content creators whose audience matches the product, reduces dependence on a paid ad budget and can establish a credibility that pure advertising doesn't achieve as easily. This channel does, however, take time before producing measurable results, which makes it less suited to quickly validating a new product than to consolidating once a product has already proven viable through other channels that are faster to test.

## Choosing the first channel to test

For a seller starting with a limited test budget, concentrating that first budget on a single channel rather than spreading it over several lets you reach enough data volume faster to draw a reliable conclusion about that specific channel, in line with Part 6's principle on reading results. Which channel to favor first depends directly on the nature of the product: a product whose appeal is visible immediately in an image is generally tested faster and more cheaply on a visual social network; a product meeting a need people already actively search for is better tested on a search engine.

## What stays true whatever channel you choose

Whatever channel you pick, two principles of this course remain constant: the acquisition cost obtained must always be related to the margin calculated in Part 3 for that specific product, never interpreted alone; and dependence on a single channel, even a very high-performing one, must be considered a risk to reduce gradually once that channel is validated, as stressed in Part 6 and repeated in the signals to watch in Part 10.
